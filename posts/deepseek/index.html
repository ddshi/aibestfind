I Almost Didn't Try DeepSeek. That Would Have Been a Mistake.

When DeepSeek exploded onto the scene, my reaction was skepticism. "Another AI chatbot? From China? That's supposedly as good as GPT-4 but free?" The claims sounded like AI hype cycle nonsense — the same breathless headlines that accompany every new model launch.

But unlike most AI hype, DeepSeek actually delivered something real. After a month of using it alongside ChatGPT Plus ($20/month) and Claude Pro ($20/month) for real work tasks, I can say this: DeepSeek isn't better than the paid options — but it's close enough to make you seriously question whether you need to pay for AI at all.

Here's what 30 days of actual use taught me.

Where DeepSeek Punches Above Its Weight

1. Coding: Genuinely Excellent

This is DeepSeek's strongest domain, and it's not close. I threw 12 different coding tasks at it — Python scripts, React components, SQL queries, Docker configurations, and one gnarly C++ memory management bug that had been haunting me for two days.

On 8 of 12 tasks, DeepSeek's solution was either the best or tied for best. Here's a concrete example: I needed a Python script that processed a 14GB CSV file in chunks (too large for memory), performed some aggregations, and output summary statistics. I asked all three AIs.

  • Claude gave me a clean, well-documented solution using pandas with chunking. It worked but was slow (~45 seconds per chunk on my machine).
  • ChatGPT suggested using dask, which was overkill for what I needed and added a dependency.
  • DeepSeek wrote a solution using the csv module with a generator pattern that processed chunks in ~2 seconds each. It included memory profiling comments and handled edge cases (malformed rows, encoding issues) that I hadn't even mentioned. This was the winner by a significant margin.

2. The 1M Token Context Window Is a Real Superpower

This deserves its own section. Most AI assistants top out at 128K-200K tokens of context. DeepSeek handles 1 million tokens — that's roughly the entire "Lord of the Rings" trilogy, or a medium-sized codebase, or a year's worth of meeting notes.

I tested this by uploading a 400-page technical documentation PDF. ChatGPT couldn't process it in one go (had to split across multiple prompts). Claude handled it but took ~30 seconds just to "read" the context. DeepSeek processed the entire document and answered questions that required cross-referencing information from pages 50 and 350 — connections that other AIs would miss because those pages would be in different chunks.

For anyone who works with large documents, legal contracts, or sprawling codebases, this alone is a reason to use DeepSeek.

3. Reasoning and Math

DeepSeek has a "Deep Think" mode (powered by their R1 reasoning model) that shows its step-by-step thought process. It's similar to what OpenAI does with o1, but it's free and you can see the full reasoning chain.

For technical problem-solving — debugging complex logic, analyzing data patterns, working through multi-step math problems — DeepSeek with R1 reasoning is excellent. It catches its own mistakes, revises assumptions, and sometimes takes 5-10 minutes to think through a hard problem before answering. The transparency of seeing the reasoning chain also builds trust in the final answer.

The Tradeoffs (And They're Real)

1. Creative Writing and Conversational Tone

This is where DeepSeek noticeably falls behind. Its writing voice is... functional. Clinical. It gets the facts right but lacks personality. When I asked all three AIs to write a blog post about AI trends, ChatGPT's version was engaging and readable; Claude's was polished and articulate; DeepSeek's was accurate but dry — like reading a well-written textbook rather than a magazine article.

If you need an AI for creative writing, marketing copy, or anything where voice and tone matter, ChatGPT still has a clear edge. DeepSeek writes like a very smart engineer — precise and correct, but not exactly charming.

2. Content Restrictions and Censorship

Let's address the elephant in the room. DeepSeek is developed by a Chinese company, and like all Chinese AI platforms, it has content restrictions. Questions about politically sensitive topics will be declined or deflected. For most professional use cases (coding, writing, analysis, research), this doesn't matter. But if your work involves topics that intersect with Chinese government sensitivities, you'll run into walls.

I tested this deliberately: questions about technology, science, business, and general knowledge were answered freely. Questions about Tiananmen Square, Taiwan independence, and certain political figures were met with "I am sorry, I cannot answer that question." This is a real limitation depending on your use case.

3. Server Congestion and Reliability

During peak hours (especially Asia daytime), DeepSeek sometimes throws "server busy" errors. In my 30-day test, this happened 4 times — not terrible, but annoying when you're in the middle of work. The infrastructure seems to be improving, but it's not as reliable as OpenAI or Anthropic's always-available service.

4. Limited Integrations and Ecosystem

ChatGPT has plugins, GPTs, DALL-E integration, code interpreter, and a thriving third-party ecosystem. Claude has Projects, artifacts, and excellent API documentation. DeepSeek is... a chat interface. It generates text (and code) really well, but there's no image generation, no browsing (though they've been testing it), no plugin marketplace, and a simpler API.

My Actual Workflow After 30 Days

I didn't replace ChatGPT and Claude entirely. Here's what actually happened:

  • Writing tasks → ChatGPT (better voice, more engaging)

  • Technical documentation → Claude (cleaner, more structured output)
  • Quick questions / casual use → DeepSeek (because it's free)

So I'm still paying for ChatGPT Plus and Claude Pro. But I'm questioning both subscriptions. If DeepSeek improves its writing quality and adds a few features, it could genuinely replace both for a lot of my work.
