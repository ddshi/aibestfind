The AI Article Writer 6.0: The Heart of Writesonic

This is the feature that's supposed to compete with tools like Jasper, Copy.ai, and even ChatGPT. I generated 12 blog posts across different niches — tech reviews, marketing guides, and lifestyle content — to stress-test it.

What the Workflow Looks Like

  1. Pick your topic — enter a keyword or headline
  2. Choose tone and format — Writesonic suggests outlines based on top-ranking content
  3. Review the AI-generated outline — you can edit, reorder, or add sections before generation
  4. Generate the full article — the AI writes section by section, pulling in data when you enable SEO mode
  5. Edit and refine — in-line editing with AI rephrasing suggestions

The entire process takes roughly 15-20 minutes per article from keyword to first draft. For comparison, my manual writing process for a 1,500-word article takes 3-4 hours. Even with heavy editing, Writesonic cuts that down to about 45 minutes.

SEO Mode: The Feature That Separates Writesonic From Generic AI Writers

Most AI writers generate decent text. Very few understand SEO. Writesonic's SEO Mode is what makes this a serious tool for anyone publishing content that needs to rank.

When you enable SEO Mode, Writesonic does three things that generic ChatGPT prompts can't replicate:

  1. Competitor content analysis — it scans the top 10 Google results for your keyword and identifies what they cover (and what they miss)
  2. Keyword integration — it suggests primary and secondary keywords and weaves them naturally into the draft, tracking keyword density as it writes
  3. Outline optimization — it compares your article structure against the top-ranking pages and flags gaps in your coverage

I tested this on a moderately competitive keyword ("best CRM for small business") and compared the Writesonic-generated draft against a manual draft I'd spent 4 hours on. The Writesonic version covered 7 out of 9 subtopics that appeared in the top 5 search results. My manual draft covered only 4 because I relied on my own knowledge rather than competitive research.

The catch: the SEO Mode only works well for English content. I tested it with a Chinese-language keyword, and the competitor analysis fell apart — it pulled English results and tried to translate them, which produced awkward, unidiomatic text.

Brand Voice: Teaching the AI to Sound Like You

Writesonic introduced Brand Voice profiles that let you define how the AI should write. You can upload existing content samples, and the AI analyzes your tone, vocabulary, sentence length, and stylistic patterns.

I uploaded 5 of my published blog posts (about 8,000 words total). The tool identified my voice as:

  • Tone: Direct, slightly informal, skeptical
  • Sentence structure: Varied, with frequent short punchy sentences between longer paragraphs
  • Vocabulary: Moderate complexity, avoids jargon unless necessary

The results were mixed. For product descriptions and ad copy, the Brand Voice feature produced output that genuinely sounded like me — to the point where a colleague couldn't tell which Facebook ads I'd written manually and which were AI-generated with Brand Voice enabled.

For long-form articles, the effect was subtler. The AI sounded more conversational than without Brand Voice, but it still didn't capture my specific humor or the way I structure arguments. It felt like a version of me edited by a cautious corporate copywriter.

ChatSonic: The Built-in AI Chatbot with Real-Time Data

ChatSonic is Writesonic's answer to ChatGPT — a conversational AI interface with a critical differentiator: real-time web access. While ChatGPT's free tier is locked to a knowledge cutoff date, ChatSonic can pull current information from the web.

I used it for three tasks:

  1. Research: "What are the top 3 AI writing tools launched in the last 6 months?" — ChatSonic returned accurate results with links to source articles
  2. Competitive analysis: "Compare Writesonic and Jasper's pricing for a team of 5" — It produced a side-by-side comparison with current (as of July 2026) pricing
  3. Fact-checking: "Is it true that Google penalizes AI-generated content?" — It summarized Google's current stance accurately, citing the March 2024 core update

ChatSonic isn't going to replace ChatGPT for deep reasoning or coding tasks, but for content research and verification, it's genuinely more useful because of the live web access. The image generation feature (via DALL-E integration) is also a nice bonus when you need a quick hero image for a blog post.

  • SEO Mode is genuinely useful — saves hours of competitive research per article

⚠️ Considerations

  • Long-form articles lack depth — good for drafts and frameworks, but requires heavy editing for publication-quality writing

  • SEO Mode is English-only — non-English keyword research doesn't work well

  • Occasional factual errors — especially with statistics, always double-check numbers and dates

Who Should Use Writesonic (And Who Shouldn't)

Writesonic is ideal for:

  • Content marketers managing multiple client blogs who need to scale output without hiring
  • Solo entrepreneurs who need to produce regular content but don't have the budget for a writer
  • SEO teams who want AI assistance with competitive research and content gap analysis
  • E-commerce businesses generating hundreds of product descriptions

Skip Writesonic if:

  • You're a professional writer or journalist whose value is in voice, not volume — ChatGPT or Claude will give you more creative freedom
  • You primarily create non-English content — the SEO features won't help you
  • You need highly technical or academic content — Writesonic's factual accuracy requires too much verification
