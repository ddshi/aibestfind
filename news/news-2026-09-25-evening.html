AI News Evening Brief: Latest AI Updates & New Tools in 2026 | 2026-09-25

Today in AI: Meta's Wearable Bet, Google's Agentic Ambitions, and a $22B Voice Juggernaut

Today's AI landscape is defined by two converging forces: Big Tech's push to embed AI agents into everyday life, and the staggering valuations flowing into AI-native startups. Meta unveiled a Tamagotchi-style wearable for its Muse agent alongside camera-free smart glasses, while Google began testing Gemini's ability to make phone calls on users' behalf. Meanwhile, ElevenLabs reportedly hit a $22 billion valuation, Lovable's annualized revenue crossed $600 million, and Anthropic claimed an early breakthrough from its biology lab. Regulatory scrutiny also intensified as Australia launched an investigation into an OpenAI-linked government website hack.

1. Meta Debuts Tamagotchi-Like Wearable and Camera-Free AI Glasses for Its Muse Agent

Meta is doubling down on ambient AI hardware with two new form factors: a pocketable, Tamagotchi-inspired device called Muse Charm that serves as a physical companion for its Muse AI agent, and a pair of camera-free AI glasses aimed at privacy-conscious users. The Charm taps into a broader trend of "AI companions" — tactile, always-present devices that make agentic AI feel personal rather than purely software-based. The camera-free glasses signal Meta is willing to sacrifice its signature visual AI features to address mounting privacy backlash.

  • Why it matters: Meta is betting that the next AI interface isn't a screen but a wearable — and that privacy concerns can be designed around rather than ignored.
  • Source: TechCrunch AI

2. Google Tests Letting Gemini Make Phone Calls on Your Behalf

Google has begun testing a feature that lets its Gemini AI agent place phone calls to businesses for users — initially limited to US Pixel owners. The capability represents a significant step toward truly agentic AI that can act in the physical world, not just answer questions. It also raises immediate questions about consent, disclosure, and whether businesses are prepared to handle AI callers.

  • Why it matters: If Gemini can reliably navigate phone trees and negotiate appointments, it transforms from an assistant into a genuine digital proxy — a major leap in AI utility.
  • Source: TechCrunch AI

3. ElevenLabs Reportedly Valued at $22B as Voice AI Demand Surges

ElevenLabs CEO Mati Staniszewski sat down for a candid 20-minute interview as reports emerged that the voice AI company has reached a staggering $22 billion valuation. The company has become the de facto standard for AI-generated speech, powering everything from audiobooks to real-time dubbing. Its meteoric rise underscores how quickly specialized AI infrastructure companies can achieve mega-cap status.

  • Why it matters: Voice is emerging as one of the most commercially viable AI modalities, and ElevenLabs' valuation signals investors see it as a category-defining platform.
  • Source: TechCrunch AI

4. Lovable's Annualized Revenue Crosses $600M as Vibe Coding Goes Mainstream

Lovable, the AI-powered coding platform that lets users build software through natural language prompts, has crossed $600 million in annualized revenue. The milestone validates the "vibe coding" movement — a paradigm where software creation is democratized beyond traditional developers. Lovable's growth trajectory suggests enterprises and hobbyists alike are embracing prompt-driven development at scale.

  • Why it matters: Vibe coding is no longer a novelty — it's a revenue-generating category, and Lovable is its clearest winner so far.
  • Source: TechCrunch AI

5. Anthropic Says Its Biology Lab Has Already Found Something Big

Anthropic announced that its dedicated biology research lab has produced an early but significant discovery, though the company stopped short of detailing the finding. The revelation positions Anthropic not just as an AI safety-focused model builder but as an active participant in scientific discovery. It also intensifies the race among AI labs to demonstrate real-world breakthroughs beyond benchmarks.

  • Why it matters: If AI labs can consistently generate novel biological insights, the intersection of AI and life sciences could become the most consequential frontier of the decade.
  • Source: TechCrunch AI

6. Australia Investigates Whether OpenAI-Linked Hack of Government Health Website Broke the Law

Australian authorities have launched an investigation into whether an OpenAI-related security incident involving a government health website violated national law. The probe raises uncomfortable questions about liability when AI systems are used to probe or exploit public infrastructure. It also signals that governments are beginning to treat AI-enabled cyber incidents with the same seriousness as traditional cyberattacks.

  • Why it matters: This case could set a global precedent for how AI companies are held accountable when their technology is implicated in unauthorized access to government systems.
  • Source: TechCrunch AI

7. Ando Builds a Slack Competitor Where Humans and AI Agents Work Side by Side

Ando is developing a team messaging platform designed from the ground up for a workforce that includes both humans and AI agents. Unlike Slack, where bots are add-ons, Ando treats agents as first-class participants with their own identities, permissions, and workflows. The startup is betting that the future of work isn't just AI-assisted — it's AI-populated.

  • Why it matters: As AI agents become more capable, the tools we use to collaborate will need to fundamentally rethink what a "team member" is.
  • Source: TechCrunch AI

8. AI Dominates the Conversation at Climate Week

At this year's Climate Week, AI was front and center — not just as a tool for climate modeling, but as a growing source of energy demand and carbon emissions. Discussions spanned AI's role in optimizing energy grids, accelerating materials discovery, and the uncomfortable tension between AI's climate benefits and its environmental costs. The MIT Technology Review's coverage captured a sector increasingly forced to reckon with its own footprint.

  • Why it matters: AI's climate narrative is shifting from "AI will save the planet" to "AI must clean up its own act first."
  • Source: MIT Tech Review AI

9. Enveda Secures $311M to Bring Nature-Derived AI Drugs into Clinical Trials

Enveda has raised $311 million to accelerate its approach of using AI to discover drugs from natural sources — plants, fungi, and other organisms. The company's platform analyzes biological data at scale to identify promising compounds that traditional drug discovery might overlook. The raise reflects continued investor appetite for AI-driven biotech, even as the sector faces long timelines and high failure rates.

  • Why it matters: Enveda represents a growing class of AI biotech companies betting that machine learning can compress the decades-long drug discovery process.
  • Source: TechCrunch AI

10. Google Photos Launches 'Clueless'-Inspired Virtual Closet on Android and iOS

Google Photos has rolled out a virtual closet feature — inspired by the cult classic film "Clueless" — that uses AI to catalog your wardrobe from photos and suggest outfits. Available now on both Android and iOS, the feature is Google's latest attempt to turn its photo library into a proactive, AI-powered lifestyle assistant. It's a playful but telling example of how AI is creeping into everyday consumer experiences.

  • Why it matters: The virtual closet is a small feature with big implications: Google is training users to let AI organize and act on their personal data.
  • Source: TechCrunch AI

Editor's Note

Today's news paints a picture of an industry simultaneously maturing and accelerating. The hardware bets from Meta, the agentic ambitions of Google, and the eye-popping valuations of ElevenLabs and Lovable all point to one conclusion: AI is no longer a feature — it's becoming the platform. At the same time, regulatory scrutiny from Australia and the climate reckoning at Climate Week remind us that this growth is not without friction. The companies that navigate both the technical and societal dimensions of this shift will define the next chapter.
