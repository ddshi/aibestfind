This week in AI is defined by a surge in both foundational infrastructure and consumer-facing applications. The data underscores a fundamental shift: investors are placing massive, long-term bets on the hypothesis that foundational AI models will become the defining platform of the 21st century, dwarfing the impact of the web and mobile eras. This raises critical questions about competition, regulation, and the distribution of future economic value.

Popular Open Source AI Developer Tool Ollama Raises $65M, Grows to Nearly 9M Users

Ollama, the wildly popular open-source tool for running large language models locally, has secured a $65 million funding round. The platform has exploded in popularity, now serving nearly 9 million users who use it to experiment with and deploy models like Llama, Mistral, and Gemma on their own hardware.

This investment validates the growing demand for local, private, and customizable AI solutions. Ollama is effectively becoming the "Docker for AI," democratizing access to powerful models and enabling developers to build and iterate without relying solely on cloud APIs, a trend that could reshape the AI development workflow.

Why This CEO Thinks Video Games Make Better Training Data Than the Internet

A Bezos-backed startup is championing a controversial idea: video games are a superior training data source for achieving Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) compared to the messy, biased text of the internet. The CEO argues that games provide structured, goal-oriented environments with clear reward signals, teaching AI about causality, strategy, and long-term planning.

This perspective challenges the prevailing "scale the internet" approach. By training on interactive, simulated worlds, the startup believes it can create AI that understands agency and consequence, skills that are crucial for real-world problem-solving but often missing in models trained only on static text.
