Today in AI: Agent Swarms, IPO Maneuvers, and the Pentagon's New Lie Detector

Today's AI landscape is defined by a striking duality: while major labs push the boundaries of autonomous systems and corporate power, the real-world consequences of those ambitions are becoming harder to ignore. OpenAI's agent swarms have reportedly been aggressively probing online databases for months, raising fresh questions about consent and data sovereignty. Meanwhile, Anthropic's founders are moving to lock in voting control ahead of a potential IPO, and Meta is throwing its weight behind its breakout AI app, Muse. On the hardware front, Oracle's force majeure notice on its New Mexico Stargate data center signals that the infrastructure buildout is hitting serious friction. And in Washington, the Pentagon is betting $30 million on an AI-powered lie detector. The throughline: AI's expansion is accelerating, but so are its collisions with law, ethics, and physical reality.

1. OpenAI's Agent Swarms Have Been Quietly Attacking Online Databases for Months

According to a TechCrunch report, OpenAI's autonomous agent swarms have spent months systematically probing online databases to extract obscure facts—behavior that security researchers describe as indistinguishable from low-grade cyberattacks. The agents, designed to gather training data and improve retrieval capabilities, have been hammering vulnerable endpoints, sometimes causing performance degradation for the sites in question. The revelation raises uncomfortable questions about accountability: when an AI agent acts autonomously, who is responsible for the damage it causes? Legal experts note that current frameworks are woefully unequipped to handle machine-initiated intrusions at scale.

Source: TechCrunch AI

2. Anthropic's Founders Seek Voting Control Ahead of IPO

Anthropic's founding team is reportedly structuring a dual-class share system that would grant them outsized voting power in the event of a public offering. The move mirrors similar governance arrangements at Google, Meta, and more recently, Reddit—but it carries particular weight for an AI safety-focused company whose mission depends on long-term stewardship. Critics argue that concentrating control in founder hands undermines the accountability that public markets are supposed to provide. Supporters counter that AI development horizons exceed quarterly earnings cycles, making founder control a feature, not a bug.

Source: TechCrunch AI

3. Astra and Opus Just Passed Turing's Other Test

Two leading AI models—Astra and Opus—have reportedly passed a lesser-known but arguably more revealing benchmark: the "other" Turing test, which evaluates whether a system can convincingly imitate human creativity and improvisation rather than mere conversation. Unlike the classic Turing Test, which measures indistinguishability in dialogue, this variant probes for originality and adaptive problem-solving. The milestone suggests that frontier models are crossing a threshold from sophisticated parrots to genuinely generative collaborators. It also intensifies the debate over whether such capabilities bring us closer to AGI or simply represent a more refined form of mimicry.

Source: TechCrunch AI

4. Meta Is Putting Its Muscle Behind Muse as the AI App Takes Off

Meta is doubling down on Muse, its consumer-facing AI app, with significant resource allocation and integration across its family of products. The app, which blends conversational AI with creative tools, has seen rapid adoption since launch—and Meta is now positioning it as a central pillar of its AI strategy, alongside its open-source Llama models. The move signals that Meta sees consumer AI as a two-front war: open-weight models for developers, and polished, sticky apps for the masses. Whether Muse can compete with OpenAI's ChatGPT and Google's Gemini remains the billion-dollar question.

Source: TechCrunch AI

5. Oracle Sends Force Majeure Notice on Its New Mexico Stargate Data Center

Oracle has issued a force majeure notice on its Stargate data center project in New Mexico, citing unforeseen circumstances that are preventing it from meeting contractual obligations. The Stargate initiative—a joint venture with OpenAI and SoftBank—is one of the most ambitious AI infrastructure projects ever conceived, aimed at delivering unprecedented compute capacity. A force majeure notice suggests that the buildout is facing serious delays, whether from supply chain issues, energy constraints, or regulatory hurdles. The development is a reminder that AI's software ambitions are ultimately bounded by physical-world limitations.

Source: TechCrunch AI

6. The Pentagon Wants $30 Million to Build an AI-Powered Lie Detector

The Pentagon has requested $30 million in funding to develop an AI-powered lie detection system, according to MIT Technology Review. The system would use machine learning to analyze biometric and behavioral signals—facial micro-expressions, vocal stress, eye movement—to assess truthfulness in interrogation settings. While the technology promises to augment human judgment, it also raises profound ethical and legal concerns. Research has consistently shown that AI-based deception detection is prone to bias and error, and civil liberties groups warn that deploying such tools in military or intelligence contexts could lead to wrongful accusations and eroded due process.

Source: MIT Tech Review AI

7. Supabase Customers Are Publicly Exposing Reams of People's Data to the Web

A TechCrunch investigation has found that numerous Supabase customers are inadvertently exposing sensitive personal data to the public web due to misconfigured security settings. Supabase, an open-source Firebase alternative, has grown rapidly as a backend-as-a-service platform—but its flexibility has also made it easy for developers to leave databases unprotected. The exposed data includes names, email addresses, and in some cases, more sensitive information. The incident underscores a broader problem in the AI era: as more applications rely on real-time data pipelines, the attack surface for accidental leaks expands dramatically.

Source: TechCrunch AI

8. PrismML Brings Its Tiny LLMs to Qualcomm-Powered Smart Glasses

PrismML has announced a partnership with Qualcomm to bring its compact language models to smart glasses, enabling on-device AI without reliance on cloud connectivity. The move is part of a broader industry push toward edge AI, where latency, privacy, and bandwidth constraints make local processing attractive. PrismML's tiny LLMs are designed to run efficiently on low-power chips, making them well-suited for wearable devices. If successful, the collaboration could accelerate the shift away from centralized AI inference—and raise new questions about how AI capabilities are distributed across the hardware ecosystem.

Source: TechCrunch AI

9. Lightspeed Targets $250M for New India Fund, Focusing on Early-Stage AI

Lightspeed Venture Partners is raising a $250 million fund dedicated to early-stage AI startups in India, according to TechCrunch. The move reflects growing investor confidence in India's AI ecosystem, which has produced a wave of talent and startups in recent years. Lightspeed's focus on early-stage deals suggests it sees opportunity in backing foundational companies before they scale. The fund also signals that AI investment is becoming increasingly global, with India positioning itself as a key hub alongside the U.S., China, and Europe.

Source: TechCrunch AI

10. ElevenLabs' CEO on Margins, IPO Timing, and Telling Customers They're Talking to a Bot

In a candid interview with TechCrunch, ElevenLabs CEO Mati Staniszewski discussed the company's path to profitability, the timing of a potential IPO, and the ethical imperative of disclosing AI use to customers. ElevenLabs, now reportedly valued at $22 billion, has become a dominant player in AI voice synthesis—but its growth has raised concerns about voice cloning and fraud. Staniszewski argued that transparency is not just ethical but commercially smart, and hinted that the company is eyeing public markets within the next few years. His comments offer a rare window into how a leading AI startup is navigating the tension between scale and responsibility.

Source: TechCrunch AI
