AI News Digest — November 27, 2026

This week's AI news is defined less by model launches than by the physical and social infrastructure AI now demands. 1. Data Centers Are Getting Their Own Natural Gas Power Plants

AI's insatiable compute demands are reshaping the American energy grid. Utilities and hyperscalers are increasingly building dedicated natural-gas plants to serve data center campuses directly, sidestepping the long interconnection queues that have slowed renewable projects. The shift marks a striking reversal: the industry that pledged to lead the clean-energy transition is now anchoring a fossil-fuel boom.

Source: MIT Technology Review

3. AI Is Straining America's Open Records Systems

Public records offices are being flooded with AI-generated requests, bogging down already under-resourced agencies and threatening the transparency laws they exist to uphold. The volume and sophistication of automated filings are forcing officials to consider new verification rules and fees. The story is an early warning about how AI agents will stress civic institutions designed for human-scale interaction.

Source: MIT Technology Review

4. A Legendary Philosopher Isn't Buying Anthropic's Consciousness Argument

Anthropic's suggestion that its models may warrant moral consideration has drawn sharp rebuttal from a prominent philosopher, who argues the company is conflating sophisticated language behavior with genuine inner experience. The exchange matters because it previews a coming policy debate: as labs make claims about model welfare, regulators and the public will need a defensible framework for evaluating them. Expect this argument to intensify as models grow more convincing.

Source: MIT Technology Review

5. The Next Generation of Bionic Hands Is Here—and They Can Feel

New prosthetic hands restore a sense of touch by feeding sensory signals back into the nervous system, a leap beyond motor-only control. The devices are also becoming research platforms, letting scientists study how the brain integrates artificial sensation. It's one of the clearest examples of AI-adjacent hardware delivering immediate, tangible human benefit.

Source: MIT Technology Review
