Top Stories

1. Anthropic’s Landmark $1.5B Copyright Settlement is Approved

In a decision that will set a precedent for the entire industry, a federal judge has approved Anthropic's massive $1.5 billion copyright settlement with a coalition of authors and publishers. The agreement, one of the largest in tech history, resolves claims that the company used copyrighted works to train its Claude models without permission. While Anthropic admits no liability, the payout signals that the cost of training on public data is finally coming due, potentially reshaping how all frontier labs approach data sourcing.

2. US Threatens Sanctions Against Chinese AI Models Over IP Theft

The Biden administration's trade office has issued a stark warning, threatening sweeping sanctions against Chinese AI models found to be using stolen American intellectual property. This escalation comes as reports surface that Chinese labs have systematically reverse-engineered proprietary architectures from U.S. frontier models. The move has deeply divided the White House, with national security hawks pushing for immediate action while economic advisors warn that a decoupling could cripple global supply chains.

Source: TechCrunch

3. Data Centers Expected to Use 4x More Electricity by 2035

A sobering new report from the International Energy Agency projects that global data center electricity consumption will quadruple by 2035, driven almost entirely by the insatiable power demands of AI training and inference. This projection puts immense pressure on Big Tech's net-zero pledges, as companies scramble to invest in small modular nuclear reactors and next-generation grid infrastructure. The finding underscores that AI's environmental footprint is no longer a niche concern but a central operational risk for the industry.

Source: TechCrunch

Rumors are swirling that Anthropic is in advanced talks to acquire or deeply partner with Physical Intelligence, the robotics AI startup. While neither company has confirmed the speculation, the chatter has sent shockwaves through the industry, suggesting that the race for "embodied AI" is heating up. If true, this would mark a major strategic pivot for Anthropic, moving from pure language models into the hardware-software stack required for robots to operate in the physical world.

Google has quietly rolled out three new Gemini model variants focused on efficiency and specific use cases, but the anticipated Gemini 3.5 Pro flagship is notably absent. The new models include a "Nano" for on-device tasks, a "Flash" optimized for real-time reasoning, and a "Ultra" for enterprise workloads. 7. Music Streamer Deezer Says More Than 50% of Daily Uploads are AI-Generated

Deezer has revealed that for the first time, over half of the tracks uploaded to its platform daily are generated entirely by AI. The company is scrambling to implement new detection and labeling systems to prevent the platform from being flooded with low-quality synthetic music. This statistic is a stark indicator of how generative AI is already reshaping the creative industries, forcing streaming services to become arbiters of authenticity.

Source: TechCrunch

Jack Dorsey's new venture, Buzz, has officially launched as a direct competitor to Slack, with a twist: it is designed from the ground up for human-AI agent collaboration. The platform features native "agent channels" where AI assistants can autonomously complete tasks, query data, and interact with human colleagues. Dorsey is betting that the future of work isn't just about humans chatting, but about humans orchestrating swarms of AI agents.

Source: TechCrunch

The app allows parents to input characters, settings, and moral lessons, and the AI generates a unique narrative complete with narration and sound effects. While the utility is clear for busy parents, critics argue it is a solution in search of a problem, further eroding the human ritual of storytelling.

The company’s robots can install solar panels at a rate ten times faster than human crews, addressing the massive labor shortage in the renewable energy sector. Gritt's long-term vision is to expand into general construction, positioning itself as a key player in the "AI for infrastructure" boom.
