AI Weekly Digest: The Week OpenAI Went to War (And Launched a Model)

This week in AI was dominated by a single, sprawling narrative: OpenAI. The company launched its next-generation GPT-5.6 model, setting the stage for a potential rift with Microsoft, while simultaneously fending off a high-stakes trade secret lawsuit from Apple and a copyright evidence dispute with the New York Times. Meanwhile, the industry grappled with the meaning of open source, the rise of autonomous AI agents, and the raw infrastructure powering it all, as SK Hynix pulled off the largest foreign IPO in U.S. history. Here are the stories that defined the week.

1. Apple Sues OpenAI Over Alleged Trade Secret Theft

In a dramatic escalation of tensions between two tech titans, Apple has filed a lawsuit against OpenAI, alleging the AI company stole trade secrets related to its on-device intelligence architecture. The suit claims that former Apple employees who joined OpenAI brought proprietary information about "Apple Neural Core," a system designed to run complex models directly on iPhone hardware. This legal action arrives just as whispers of a "war" between the two companies over talent and AI strategy reach a fever pitch.

Source: TechCrunch AI

2. OpenAI Launches GPT-5.6, Names It the 'Preferred Model' for Microsoft Copilot

OpenAI officially unveiled its new family of models with GPT-5.6, which it has designated as the "preferred model" for Microsoft Copilot 365. The announcement comes amid persistent chatter about a potential "breakup" or renegotiation of the exclusive partnership between the two companies. While the model boasts significant improvements in reasoning and context length, the strategic messaging suggests OpenAI is trying to lock in its relationship with Microsoft even as it explores building its own consumer "super app."

Source: TechCrunch AI

3. SK Hynix Raises $26.5B in Historic U.S. IPO, Urged to Build New Fabs

SK Hynix has shattered records by raising $26.5 billion in the largest foreign initial public offering in U.S. history. The memory chip giant, whose high-bandwidth memory (HBM) is critical for Nvidia's AI accelerators, is now facing intense political pressure to build new fabrication plants on American soil. The IPO underscores the reality that the AI boom is fundamentally a hardware story, with the companies that manufacture the physical components of intelligence commanding enormous capital.

Source: TechCrunch AI

4. Anthropic Discovers a 'Hidden Space' Where Claude Puzzles Over Concepts

In a breakthrough for AI interpretability, Anthropic researchers have identified a previously unknown "hidden space" within the Claude model where it appears to process and puzzle over abstract concepts before outputting a response. This internal reasoning layer, which operates in a latent semantic space, offers a rare window into how large language models actually "think." The finding could have profound implications for safety research, as it may allow engineers to see when a model is uncertain or wrestling with conflicting information.

Source: MIT Technology Review

5. An AI Agent Startup Just Let Its Agent Run Its $100M Fundraise

In a meta moment that perfectly captures the current hype cycle, an AI agent startup revealed that it allowed one of its own autonomous agents to manage the entire $100 million fundraise process. The agent reportedly drafted the pitch deck, scheduled investor meetings, and even negotiated term sheets—allegedly securing better terms than the human founders could have. While the story is likely part marketing stunt, it highlights the rapid maturation of agentic AI and the blurring line between tool and decision-maker.

Source: TechCrunch AI

6. New York Times Says OpenAI Hid Evidence in ChatGPT Copyright Trial

The legal battle between the New York Times and OpenAI took a nasty turn this week, with the newspaper accusing OpenAI of deliberately hiding evidence related to the training data used for ChatGPT. The Times alleges that OpenAI failed to produce internal documents showing how copyrighted articles were ingested and used to generate responses. The accusation, if proven, could severely damage OpenAI's credibility in court and set a dangerous precedent for discovery in AI copyright cases.

Source: TechCrunch AI

7. Meta Removes Controversial AI Feature on Instagram After Backlash

Meta has pulled a controversial AI feature from Instagram following a massive user backlash. The feature, which automatically generated "memory reels" using user photos and AI-generated narration, was criticized for creating emotionally manipulative content that users did not consent to. The removal marks a rare retreat for Meta, which has been aggressively pushing generative AI features across its social platforms, and signals growing user resistance to AI-driven personalization that feels intrusive.

Source: TechCrunch AI

8. Hugging Face CEO: 'Companies Are Done Renting Their AI'

Hugging Face CEO Clem Delangue made a compelling case this week for the resurgence of open-source AI, arguing that enterprises are increasingly rejecting the "rental" model of proprietary API-based AI. Delangue cited concerns over vendor lock-in, data privacy, and cost as driving a wave of companies choosing to self-host or fine-tune open models. The argument is bolstered by the growing availability of powerful open-source models that can rival GPT-4 in specific domains, reshaping the economic calculus of enterprise AI adoption.

Source: TechCrunch AI

9. Fidji Simo Steps Down from OpenAI's No. 2 Role

Fidji Simo, the former Instacart CEO who joined OpenAI as its Chief Operating Officer, has stepped down from the company's second-highest leadership position. Her departure adds to a pattern of high-level executive turnover at the company, which has seen several key leaders exit in recent months. While OpenAI cited personal reasons for the departure, the move raises questions about stability at the top of the world's most valuable AI startup as it navigates multiple lawsuits and a rapidly shifting competitive landscape.

Source: TechCrunch AI

10. Google Will Now Disclose Which Ads Are Made with AI

Google has announced a new policy requiring advertisers to disclose when their ads are generated or significantly modified using AI tools. The move is part of a broader industry push toward transparency, as regulators and consumers grow increasingly concerned about deepfakes and AI-generated misinformation in advertising. The policy applies to both image and video ads, and Google has warned that non-compliance could result in ad suspension, setting a new standard for accountability in digital marketing.

Source: TechCrunch AI
