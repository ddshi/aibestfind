AI News Digest — November 10, 2026

Today's AI landscape is dominated by Anthropic's blockbuster IPO, which is set to mint one of the most valuable companies in tech history and shower its founding team with staggering personal windfalls. Beyond the markets, the product race intensified across productivity, creative tools, and wearables, with Claude, ChatGPT, Adobe, and Meta all shipping new capabilities. Meanwhile, Google expanded its custom silicon ambitions, and troubling questions continued to swirl around Nvidia's leadership. Here are the day's most significant stories, ranked by impact.

1. Anthropic Sets IPO Price Range of $70–$78 Per Share, Implying a $1.404 Trillion Valuation

Anthropic has formally set the terms of its long-anticipated initial public offering, targeting a price band of $70 to $78 per share that would value the company at an extraordinary $1.404 trillion. The figure places the Claude maker in rarefied territory alongside the world's most valuable technology firms, underscoring how dramatically enterprise demand for frontier AI models has reshaped investor expectations. The pricing signals that public markets are prepared to reward AI leaders at scale, even as questions about sustained profitability across the sector remain unresolved.

Source: TechCrunch AI

2. All Seven Anthropic Founders to Cash Out Roughly $250M Each in IPO

In a detail that underscores the scale of the offering, all seven of Anthropic's co-founders are each positioned to liquidate approximately $250 million in shares as part of the IPO. The collective payout — north of $1.75 billion among the founding group alone — represents one of the largest single wealth-creation events in AI history. It also raises familiar governance questions about insider selling at the moment of going public, particularly for a company whose stated mission centers on the safe and responsible development of AI.

Source: TechCrunch AI

3. Anthropic Launches Claude Meetings to Turn Workplace Discussions into Plans and Tasks

Anthropic introduced Claude Meetings, a new capability that ingests workplace discussions and converts them into structured plans, action items, and assigned tasks. The move pushes Claude deeper into the productivity suite wars, directly challenging established players in meeting intelligence such as Otter and Microsoft's Copilot ecosystem. It also signals Anthropic's intent to compete on everyday enterprise workflows, not just raw model capability, as it prepares to face public-market scrutiny.

Source: TechCrunch AI

4. Google Broadens AI Hardware Portfolio With Two New Specialized Chips

Google expanded its custom silicon lineup with two new specialized AI chips, deepening its effort to reduce reliance on third-party accelerators and optimize its infrastructure for specific workloads. The announcement reinforces Google's vertically integrated strategy — pairing in-house TPUs with its Gemini models and cloud platform — as competition over AI compute economics intensifies. Specialized silicon is increasingly viewed as a decisive lever for cost control and performance differentiation at hyperscale.

Source: TechCrunch AI

5. Adobe Brings AI Agents and New Creative Tools to Photoshop, Premiere, and Firefly — and Launches an MCP

Adobe rolled out a broad AI update across its flagship creative applications, embedding agentic capabilities into Photoshop, Premiere, and Firefly, and launching its own implementation of the Model Context Protocol (MCP). The MCP support is particularly notable, positioning Adobe's tools to interoperate with a growing ecosystem of external AI agents and services. For Adobe, the play is twofold: defend its creative franchise against fast-moving AI-native rivals while making its suite the connective tissue for agent-driven content workflows.

Source: TechCrunch AI

6. OpenAI Rolls Out Business Profiles, Making ChatGPT a Little More Like Google

OpenAI introduced business profiles within ChatGPT, giving companies a structured presence in the assistant and nudging the product closer to a search-and-discovery surface. The feature opens a potential new advertising and commerce channel for OpenAI while giving brands a way to surface authoritative information directly to users. It's the clearest signal yet that ChatGPT is evolving from a pure conversational tool into a platform with its own discoverability layer.

Source: TechCrunch AI

7. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's Alleged Affair With a Suspected Chinese Spy: A Timeline

A detailed timeline has emerged tracing Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's alleged relationship with a person suspected of being a Chinese spy, a story with significant implications for the chipmaker at the center of the AI boom. Reporting indicates a short seller flagged the ties roughly six months before the scandal broke, and that Huang engaged a PR firm with experience managing high-profile sex scandals a year prior. The controversy raises serious questions about national security, corporate governance, and disclosure at one of the world's most systemically important technology companies.

Source: TechCrunch AI

8. Oura to Acquire AI-Native Primary Care Startup Counsel Health for $625M

Wearables maker Oura agreed to acquire Counsel Health, an AI-native primary care startup, for $625 million — a striking move that extends the company from sensor hardware into clinical services. The deal reflects a broader convergence of consumer health devices and AI-delivered care, where continuous biometric data can feed personalized medical guidance. It also signals that the next competitive frontier in wearables may be services and outcomes rather than hardware specs alone.

Source: TechCrunch AI

9. Meta's AI Agent Muse Lands on Apple Watch and Wear OS

Meta's AI agent Muse is now available on Apple Watch and Android Wear OS, extending the assistant beyond the phone and into wrist-worn, glanceable interactions. The expansion puts Meta in direct competition with platform-native assistants on devices it doesn't control, a notable strategic gamble. It also hints at Meta's ambition to make Muse a cross-platform layer for everyday queries and tasks rather than a feature confined to its own apps.

Source: TechCrunch AI

10. Cyberattacks Threaten to Make Open Source AI a Little Less Open, Hugging Face Exec Warns

A Hugging Face executive warned that mounting cyberattacks against open source AI infrastructure could push the ecosystem toward tighter access controls and gated releases. The concern strikes at the heart of the open-weights movement, where unrestricted distribution has fueled rapid innovation but also created security and abuse risks. How the community balances openness against safety will shape the competitive dynamics between open and closed model developers in the year ahead.
