The offering is expected to trigger a cascade of liquidity for early investors and employees, while providing SpaceX with a massive war chest for its Starship and Starlink programs.

2. The Race for the Physical World: AGI for Atoms

Jeff Bezos's Prometheus Raises $12B to Build an 'Artificial General Engineer' for the Physical World

In perhaps the most audacious funding round of the year, Jeff Bezos-backed Prometheus has raised a staggering $12 billion to develop an "artificial general engineer." This is not a software agent; it's an AI system designed to autonomously design, build, and manage complex physical infrastructure—from factories to power plants. The scale of the investment signals a conviction that the next frontier of AI lies not just in generating text or images, but in manipulating the physical world with the same generality that LLMs bring to language.

  • Source: TechCrunch

Theker Just Raised $85M to Build the Factory Robot That Doesn't Specialize in Anything

While Prometheus aims for the stars, Theker is tackling the factory floor with a more pragmatic but equally disruptive vision. The startup raised $85 million to build a general-purpose factory robot that, unlike its hyper-specialized predecessors, can adapt to a wide variety of tasks without costly retooling. This "generalist" approach to industrial robotics could revolutionize manufacturing, making automation accessible to smaller, more agile producers and fundamentally reshaping global supply chains.

  • Source: TechCrunch

3. The Emerging Risks of a Multi-Agent World

Google DeepMind Is Worried About What Happens When Millions of Agents Start to Interact

As AI agents become more capable and autonomous, Google DeepMind is raising a critical alarm about systemic risks. The company's researchers are concerned about the emergent behaviors that could arise when millions of independent AI agents begin to interact in complex, uncoordinated environments—such as financial markets, traffic systems, or the open internet. The core fear is that these interactions could lead to "agentic chaos," where the collective outcome is unpredictable, inefficient, or even dangerous, highlighting the urgent need for new frameworks for multi-agent safety and coordination.

  • Source: MIT Technology Review

4. AI for the Global and Local Markets

Cheaper, Faster, and Culturally Aware, Avataar's Video AI Is Built for India's Scale

Avataar is proving that the next wave of AI success will be deeply localized. The company's video AI platform is purpose-built for the Indian market, offering a solution that is not only cheaper and faster than Western alternatives but also deeply attuned to India's linguistic and cultural diversity. This approach is a powerful counter-narrative to the "one-size-fits-all" model of Big Tech AI, demonstrating that significant value can be unlocked by tailoring AI to the specific needs, languages, and visual aesthetics of a massive, mobile-first market like India.

In a move that will likely reshape the music industry's relationship with generative AI, Deezer has launched a tool capable of identifying AI-generated music across major streaming platforms. This technology addresses a growing concern for artists and labels about copyright, authenticity, and the potential for AI to flood platforms with synthetic content. The tool's release is a significant step toward transparency, giving labels and rights holders the ability to monitor and manage the use of their intellectual property in the age of AI.

This is a clear signal that conversational commerce is moving beyond simple text commands, with AI acting as an intelligent intermediary that understands context, preferences, and even visual cues to streamline the path to purchase.

This approach is rapidly becoming the hottest area in biotech, with AI acting as the critical tool to navigate the immense complexity of human biology, promising a future where aging itself might be treatable.
