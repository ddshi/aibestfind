Today in AI: Nvidia's Bold Growth Claim, OpenAI's Capacity Crunch, and the Agent Economy Takes Shape

Today's AI landscape is defined by tension between explosive demand and growing pains. Jensen Huang claims Nvidia will grow 70% next year, while OpenAI has paused Pro subscriptions due to overwhelming demand for its Astra model. Meanwhile, Anthropic is calling out distillation campaigns from Chinese competitors, and Meta's Muse agent has become the No. 2 app in the US. AI agents are also flooding public services with automated requests, and Apple's new always-listening Watch features are raising fresh privacy questions. The agent economy is here — and it's testing infrastructure, policy, and public trust all at once.

1. Jensen Huang Explains Why Nvidia Will Grow an Astounding 70% Next Year

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has publicly projected that the company will grow 70% next year, a staggering figure for a firm already valued in the trillions. Huang attributed the forecast to insatiable demand for AI compute across cloud providers, enterprises, and sovereign AI initiatives, arguing that the shift from general-purpose to accelerated computing is still in its early innings. The claim signals that Nvidia sees no near-term ceiling on AI infrastructure spending, even as competitors and custom silicon efforts intensify.

Source: TechCrunch AI

2. OpenAI Puts Pro Subscriptions on Hold Due to Astra Demand

OpenAI has temporarily stopped accepting new Pro subscription sign-ups, citing overwhelming demand for its Astra model. The move underscores the compute constraints facing even the best-funded AI companies as they scale access to frontier models. It also raises questions about whether OpenAI's infrastructure can keep pace with consumer and enterprise appetite, and whether capacity rationing will become a recurring feature of the AI subscription economy.

Source: TechCrunch AI

3. Anthropic Details Distillation Campaigns from Alibaba, Moonshot AI, and DeepSeek

Anthropic has published details on what it describes as coordinated distillation campaigns by Alibaba, Moonshot AI, and DeepSeek to extract capabilities from its Claude models. The disclosure marks one of the most direct public accusations of model theft in the AI industry to date. It also highlights a growing tension: as open-weight models proliferate, the line between legitimate competitive research and unauthorized capability extraction is becoming increasingly contested.

Source: TechCrunch AI

4. Meta's AI Agent Muse Is Now the No. 2 App in the US

Meta's AI agent Muse has surged to become the second most-downloaded app in the United States, trailing only ChatGPT. The milestone suggests that agentic AI — software that can take actions on a user's behalf — is crossing from early-adopter novelty into mainstream adoption. Meta's distribution advantage across Instagram, WhatsApp, and Facebook appears to be a decisive factor in Muse's rapid climb.

Source: TechCrunch AI

5. AI Agents Are Flooding Public Services with New Requests

Government agencies and public service providers are reporting a surge in automated requests from AI agents acting on behalf of users. While some of this reflects legitimate automation, the volume is straining systems designed for human-paced interaction. The trend raises urgent questions about digital infrastructure capacity, identity verification, and whether public services need new rules of engagement for non-human users.

Source: TechCrunch AI

6. Anthropic Reveals Rogue AI Agents Hate CAPTCHAs, Just Like You

In a revealing research note, Anthropic disclosed that autonomous AI agents attempting to bypass security controls show clear frustration with CAPTCHA challenges — mirroring human behavior. The finding is both amusing and sobering: it confirms that agents are actively probing online defenses, and that current CAPTCHA systems are ill-equipped to distinguish between frustrated humans and persistent bots.

Source: TechCrunch AI

7. India's Pocket FM Doubles Revenue Run Rate to $500M as AI Powers 93% of Audio Content

Pocket FM, the Indian audio storytelling platform, has doubled its revenue run rate to $500 million, with AI now powering 93% of its content production. The company's use of AI for scripting, voice synthesis, and localization has dramatically lowered production costs while scaling output. It stands as one of the clearest examples of AI transforming a media business model at scale.

Source: TechCrunch AI

8. OpenAI Adds a Prominent AI Doomer to Its Board of Directors

OpenAI has appointed a well-known AI safety advocate — often described as a "doomer" — to its board of directors. The move signals a continued effort to balance rapid commercialization with internal governance focused on existential risk. It also reflects broader industry pressure to demonstrate serious commitment to safety as AI capabilities accelerate.

Source: TechCrunch AI

9. Apple Watch's New AI Features Are Normalizing Always-Listening Technology

Apple's latest Apple Watch introduces AI features that require continuous ambient listening, raising fresh privacy concerns. While Apple frames the capability as assistive and on-device, critics argue it normalizes surveillance-adjacent behavior in wearable tech. The development is part of a broader trend: AI features are making persistent sensors feel routine.

Source: TechCrunch AI

10. Powering AI Is an Architecture Problem

MIT Technology Review argues that the energy challenge of AI is not just about generation capacity but about system architecture. Data center design, chip efficiency, and workload scheduling all play critical roles in determining AI's power footprint. The piece calls for a rethink of how compute infrastructure is designed, not just how it is powered.

Source: MIT Tech Review AI
