AI News Digest — November 20, 2026

Today's AI landscape is defined less by a single breakthrough than by the widening reach of the technology into unexpected domains. A startup claims its AI "nose" can match human olfaction, while researchers pursue the far more speculative goal of cognitive immortality. Meanwhile, MIT Technology Review's AI Hype Index warns that the gap between marketing and reality is growing, and a major update to one of AI's most misunderstood graphs offers a timely reminder that the field's foundational metrics deserve scrutiny. — Editorial Desk

1. This Smell Startup Says Its AI Nose Is as Good as a Human's

Osmo, a startup building machine olfaction, claims its AI "nose" can match human scent perception — a milestone that, if verified, would push AI beyond the visual and auditory domains where it has already made deep inroads. Digitizing smell has long been a white whale for researchers because scent molecules are far more complex and context-dependent than pixels or phonemes. A credible AI nose could have sweeping implications for food safety, medical diagnostics, environmental monitoring, and fragrance development.

Source: MIT Technology Review

MIT Technology Review's AI Hype Index delivers a sobering read on a market where "AI-powered" has become a default label slapped onto everything from enterprise software to household appliances. The index tracks the widening gap between promotional claims and demonstrable capability, offering a useful reality check for investors and buyers alike. As the hype cycle matures, the ability to distinguish genuine capability from marketing gloss is becoming a core competency for decision-makers.

Source: MIT Technology Review

4. Inside the Quest to Make Our Minds Immortal

MIT Technology Review's latest feature explores "cognitive immortality" — the ambition to preserve, replicate, or extend human minds through technology. The piece examines both the scientific frontiers and the ethical thickets involved, from memory mapping to digital preservation of identity. It is a story as much about human aspiration as about technical feasibility, and it raises uncomfortable questions about what "self" means when it can be copied.

Source: MIT Technology Review

In a striking biomedical milestone, researchers kept a human liver alive outside the body for seven weeks — a record that could reshape organ transplantation logistics and drug testing. While not an AI story per se, the achievement sits at the intersection of bioengineering and computational modeling that increasingly informs medical research. It represents one of the week's most concrete advances in extending human biological capability beyond natural limits.

A billion-dollar battery deal involving Sila and Panasonic signals intensifying momentum in the US energy storage supply chain. For AI watchers, the story matters because compute expansion is now inseparable from energy infrastructure.

8. Hydropower Hit Record Lows in the US Southwest This Summer

Drought conditions pushed hydropower generation in the US Southwest to record lows this summer, according to new reporting on the Colorado River basin. The finding compounds energy reliability concerns precisely as AI data centers drive up electricity demand. It is a reminder that the AI boom's power needs are colliding with climate-driven constraints on existing generation.

Source: MIT Technology Review
