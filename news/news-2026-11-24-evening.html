AI News Digest — November 24, 2026

Today's AI landscape is defined by a collision between ambition and accountability. On one side, the infrastructure race is accelerating in unexpected directions — data centers are now commissioning their own natural gas power plants to keep pace with compute demand. On the other, the intellectual and ethical questions surrounding AI are growing sharper: a legendary philosopher is publicly challenging Anthropic's consciousness claims, and researchers are warning that AI's judgment problem may be more intractable than its capability gaps. Meanwhile, the AI hype cycle continues to saturate everything from battery deals to biotech, prompting fresh scrutiny of where the real value lies.

1. Data Centers Are Getting Their Own Natural Gas Power Plants

The AI compute boom is pushing data center operators to build dedicated natural gas power plants, a significant shift in how the industry sources energy. This move underscores the enormous and growing electricity demands of AI infrastructure, which is outpacing what existing grids can reliably supply. It also raises uncomfortable questions about the climate implications of AI's expansion, as companies that publicly commit to sustainability targets simultaneously invest in fossil fuel generation to keep their operations running.

The critique challenges the notion that current AI systems possess or approach any meaningful form of consciousness, and it questions the framing Anthropic has used to discuss the inner lives of its models. The intervention matters because how the industry talks about machine consciousness shapes both public perception and the regulatory environment that will govern AI development.

MIT Technology Review's daily briefing highlights two converging concerns: AI's persistent judgment problem and the unintended agricultural consequences of carbon removal at scale. The judgment issue speaks to a fundamental limitation — AI systems can generate fluent outputs but still struggle with the nuanced, context-dependent decisions that humans make routinely. 4. Why Using Rocks for Carbon Removal Could Eventually Reduce Crop Growth

Enhanced rock weathering — spreading crushed rocks on farmland to absorb CO2 — is one of the most touted carbon removal strategies, but new analysis suggests it could eventually suppress crop growth. The mechanism involves changes to soil chemistry that, over time, may reduce the availability of nutrients plants need. The finding is a cautionary tale for the carbon removal industry and a reminder that climate interventions require long-horizon ecological modeling, not just short-term carbon accounting.

Source: MIT Tech Review AI

8. A Human Liver Has Been Kept Alive Outside the Body for a Record Seven Weeks

Researchers have kept a human liver alive outside the body for a record seven weeks, a milestone with significant implications for transplantation and drug testing. The achievement relies on advanced perfusion and monitoring systems that mimic the conditions the organ experiences inside a living body. While not an AI story per se, the breakthrough exemplifies the broader wave of bioengineering advances that are increasingly intertwined with computational modeling and machine learning-driven control systems.
