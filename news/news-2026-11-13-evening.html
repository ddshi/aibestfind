AI News Digest: November 12, 2026

Today's AI landscape is dominated by a single, seismic story: the abrupt departure of Jensen Huang from Nvidia amid a spiraling scandal involving alleged affairs, a suspected Chinese spy, and a "scheme" with President Trump. The fallout is reshaping the industry in real time—raising existential questions about the future of open-source AI, triggering a congressional investigation, and sparking a leadership vacuum at the world's most valuable chipmaker. Beyond the Nvidia drama, we track a major neocloud spending spree, Google's latest Search upgrade, a significant AI security breach, and United Airlines' decision to bar AI agents from booking flights.

1. The Nvidia Crisis: Huang Out, Investigation Launched, Congress Summons Testimony

Democrats Launch Nvidia Investigation, Write Scathing Letter on Huang's 'Scheme' with Trump

Congressional Democrats have opened a formal investigation into Nvidia, accusing former CEO Jensen Huang of orchestrating a "scheme" with President Donald Trump to circumvent export controls and funnel advanced AI chips to restricted markets. The letter to Nvidia's interim leadership demands internal communications and details on Huang's dealings with the White House. The probe significantly escalates the political and legal peril facing the chipmaker.

Source: TechCrunch AI

Jensen Huang Agrees to Testify Before Congress

In a dramatic turn, Jensen Huang has agreed to testify before Congress regarding his alleged dealings with the Trump administration and the circumstances of his departure from Nvidia. The testimony is expected to be one of the most closely watched tech hearings in years, with potential implications for U.S.-China tech policy and Nvidia's export licensing. A date has not yet been set.

Source: TechCrunch AI

Jensen Huang Is Out at Nvidia — Who Is Colette Kress, His Interim Replacement?

Nvidia's board has named Colette Kress, the company's long-serving CFO, as interim CEO following Huang's departure. Kress, a financial operator with deep institutional knowledge of Nvidia's balance sheet, is seen as a stabilizing force. However, her lack of product and engineering leadership experience raises questions about whether she can steer the company through its most turbulent period.

Source: TechCrunch AI

The Unfolding Jensen Huang Scandal: A Timeline

TechCrunch has compiled a comprehensive timeline of the events leading to Huang's ouster, from the initial reports of an affair with a woman alleged to be a Chinese intelligence asset to the revelation of a third affair and the Trump connection. The timeline traces how a personal scandal metastasized into a corporate and geopolitical crisis within days. It's essential reading for anyone trying to make sense of the fastest-moving story in tech.

Source: TechCrunch AI

Jensen Huang Was Having a Third Affair, Reuters Reports

Adding to the mounting allegations, Reuters reports that Huang was engaged in a third extramarital affair, further eroding his standing and complicating Nvidia's internal response. The report has intensified calls for a full independent review of Huang's conduct and the board's knowledge of it. Nvidia has declined to comment on the specifics of the allegations.

Source: TechCrunch AI

2. The Open-Source Reckoning: Nvidia's Commitment in Doubt

Jensen Huang's Exit Puts Nvidia's Support for Open Source in Question

Huang was a vocal—if pragmatic—supporter of open-source AI frameworks, and his departure throws Nvidia's future commitment into doubt. Insiders suggest that without Huang's personal advocacy, Nvidia's board may pivot toward a more proprietary, vertically integrated software strategy. The open-source community is bracing for a potential shift in Nvidia's licensing and contribution policies.

Source: TechCrunch AI

Hugging Face CEO Vows to Keep Fighting for Open Source AI After Huang's Exit

Hugging Face CEO Clem Delangue has publicly pledged to redouble the company's open-source advocacy in the wake of Huang's departure, calling the moment "a critical inflection point for the entire AI ecosystem." Delangue warned that a retreat from open-source principles by hardware leaders could entrench a handful of closed-model gatekeepers. His statement signals that the open-source community is preparing for a political and commercial fight.

Source: TechCrunch AI

Five Ways Jensen Huang's Ouster from Nvidia Could Damage the AI Industry

TechCrunch's analysis outlines five concrete risks: a leadership vacuum at the industry's most important supplier, a potential freeze on advanced chip roadmaps, the politicization of export controls, a chilling effect on founder-led AI companies, and a loss of strategic clarity for the entire supply chain. The piece argues that Huang's departure is not just a corporate governance story—it's an industry-wide stability risk.

Source: TechCrunch AI

3. Nvidia's Chip Tracking: The Smuggling Question

Nvidia Privately Confirmed It Could Track Chips to Fight Smuggling, Even as Industry Publicly Questioned Feasibility

According to MIT Technology Review, Nvidia privately acknowledged to U.S. officials that it possesses the technical capability to track individual AI chips and trace smuggling routes, even as industry lobbyists publicly argued such tracking was impractical. The revelation undercuts Nvidia's public defense and adds a new layer of scrutiny to the congressional investigation. It also raises uncomfortable questions about what the company knew and when.

Source: MIT Tech Review AI

4. Infrastructure & Investment: The Neocloud Bet and a $4B Fund

Nebius Tests the Neocloud Playbook with $40 Billion in Planned Spending

AI cloud provider Nebius has unveiled a staggering $40 billion infrastructure roadmap, betting that demand for GPU-accelerated compute will remain insatiable despite Nvidia's leadership turmoil. The plan includes new data centers across Europe and the U.S. and long-term chip supply agreements. It's a high-stakes validation of the "neocloud" model—and a test of whether customers will commit to non-hyperscaler providers amid industry uncertainty.

Source: TechCrunch AI

Norwest Pushes Deeper into AI with New $4B Fund

Venture firm Norwest has closed a $4 billion fund dedicated to AI investments, signaling that institutional capital remains bullish on the sector despite the Nvidia shock. The fund will target early- and growth-stage AI startups across infrastructure, applications, and vertical SaaS. Norwest's move suggests that top-tier VCs see the current turmoil as a buying opportunity rather than a reason to retreat.

Source: TechCrunch AI

Google's Latest Search Upgrade Promises Better Answers to Your Most Complicated Questions

Google has rolled out a significant upgrade to its AI-powered Search experience, with improved multi-step reasoning and the ability to synthesize answers from complex, multi-part queries. The update appears designed to fend off competition from AI-native search startups and to deepen Gemini's integration into the world's most-used search engine. Early tests suggest a meaningful improvement in answer quality for technical and research-oriented queries.

Source: TechCrunch AI

Muse Can Now Place Calls to US Businesses — If You Pay

AI assistant Muse has launched a paid feature that allows it to place real phone calls to U.S. businesses on behalf of users—booking appointments, checking availability, and navigating IVR systems. The move pushes Muse into the contentious territory of AI voice agents, where regulatory and consumer-protection questions remain unresolved. It's a bold product bet that could differentiate Muse in a crowded assistant market.

Source: TechCrunch AI

Nectar Launches an AI Agent to Automate Creator Discovery and Outreach for Brands

Nectar has launched an AI agent designed to automate influencer discovery, vetting, and outreach for brand marketing teams. The agent scans social platforms, scores creators against brand-fit criteria, and initiates personalized outreach at scale. It's part of a broader wave of vertical AI agents targeting the $250 billion creator economy—and a signal that agentic automation is moving decisively into marketing workflows.

6. Security, Policy & Industry Pushback

Cyberattack on Fireworks AI Allowed Hackers to Access Notion Customers' Chat Transcripts

A cyberattack on AI inference provider Fireworks AI has exposed chat transcripts belonging to Notion customers, highlighting the cascading security risks in the AI supply chain. The breach raises serious questions about data isolation and third-party risk management for companies that rely on external inference providers. It's a stark reminder that AI infrastructure security is still maturing—and that the consequences of failure extend well beyond the breached vendor.

Source: TechCrunch AI

United Airlines Blocks AI Agents from Booking Flights, Citing Cybersecurity Risks

United Airlines has moved to block AI agents from booking flights on its platform, citing cybersecurity and fraud concerns. The decision is one of the first concrete examples of a major consumer-facing company erecting barriers to agentic AI, and it could set a precedent for how industries handle autonomous agents. It also exposes a growing tension: AI companies are racing to deploy agents, while the platforms they need to interact with are locking them out.

Source: TechCrunch AI

SoftBank's Billion-Dollar Loss May Have Put a Cap on AI's Natural Gas Ambitions

SoftBank's billion-dollar loss on its AI-related energy investments is casting doubt on the industry's push to build natural-gas-fired power plants dedicated to data centers. The loss signals that the financial math for AI-dedicated energy infrastructure is more fragile than proponents claimed, and it may slow the wave of gas-plant announcements tied to AI compute buildouts. Energy has become the quiet bottleneck of the AI boom—and SoftBank's stumble is a warning sign.

Source: TechCrunch AI
