AI News Digest — November 13, 2026

Today's AI landscape is dominated by a single, sprawling story: the unraveling scandal surrounding Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, which has metastasized from personal allegations into a full-blown political and strategic crisis for the world's most valuable chipmaker. Democrats have opened an investigation, questions mount over chip smuggling and export controls, and the company's commitment to open source hangs in the balance. Meanwhile, the commercial AI race marches on: LangChain hits a $3B valuation, ChatGPT rolls out agentic shopping, and Google sharpens its Search answers. Here are the stories that matter most.

1. The Jensen Huang Scandal Goes Political: Democrats Launch Nvidia Investigation

The crisis engulfing Nvidia escalated sharply as congressional Democrats opened a formal investigation into the company and sent a scathing letter alleging a "scheme" between Huang and former President Trump. The move transforms what began as a personal scandal into a systemic governance and national-security question for the chip giant. With Nvidia's export-control posture already under scrutiny, the investigation raises the stakes for the entire AI hardware supply chain.

  • The probe signals that Nvidia's political risk is now material, not merely reputational.
  • Expect heightened volatility across semiconductor and AI infrastructure stocks in the coming weeks.

Source: TechCrunch AI

2. Export-Banned Nvidia Chips Power China's Most Advanced AI Models

In a deeply awkward revelation for Nvidia, reporting indicates that export-banned chips sit behind China's most powerful AI models — undercutting the premise of U.S. export controls. The finding suggests smuggling or diversion channels have been effective at scale, directly undermining Washington's containment strategy. It also hands critics of the current policy framework a powerful piece of evidence as the Huang investigation unfolds.

  • The disclosure raises urgent questions about the enforceability of chip export restrictions.
  • It compounds Nvidia's regulatory exposure at the worst possible moment.

Source: TechCrunch AI

3. Nvidia Privately Confirmed It Could Track Chips — While Publicly Doubting It

According to MIT Technology Review, Nvidia privately confirmed it had the technical capability to track chips and combat smuggling, even as industry voices publicly questioned the feasibility of such tracking. The gap between the private and public positions is a credibility problem that could feature prominently in the congressional investigation. It also suggests the company had more leverage over illicit flows than it let on.

  • The private-public contradiction could become a central line of inquiry for lawmakers.

The outcome will shape the balance of power between open and closed AI development for years to come.

  • Nvidia's open-source posture has been a pillar of the broader AI ecosystem's momentum.

The first is a governance crisis: Nvidia, the company at the center of the AI boom, is now navigating a congressional probe, credible evidence of export-control evasion, and a leadership vacuum — all while its open-source posture and market dominance hang in the balance.
