Today in AI: Physical Intelligence, Open-Weight Models, and the Self-Driving Company

Today's AI landscape is defined by a push toward embodiment and autonomy. Nvidia veterans argue physical AI is broader than robotics, while Meta ships an open-weight model that runs locally on Macs. Enterprise adoption accelerates with Vercel's fastest-growing AI model and Replit's vision of the "self-driving company." Meanwhile, Anthropic opens its doors to safety researchers, Amazon reimagines the app paradigm, and AI agents drive into Rivian's vehicles. The throughline: AI is moving from chat windows into the physical world, the enterprise stack, and the devices we use daily.

1. Meta Launches Open-Weight AI Model That Runs on Macs

Meta released a new open-weight model designed to run locally on Apple Silicon Macs, signaling a continued bet on on-device AI. The move lowers the barrier for developers and privacy-conscious users who want capable AI without cloud dependency. It also intensifies competition with other open-weight players and pressures closed-model providers on pricing and accessibility.

Source: TechCrunch AI

2. Anthropic Grants METR Researchers Employee-Level Access for Safety Audits

Anthropic is allowing METR researchers to look "under the hood" with employee-level access, an unusual transparency move in the race to build safe frontier models. The arrangement gives independent evaluators deeper visibility into model behavior, training, and deployment decisions. It sets a new precedent for third-party safety auditing that other labs may be pressured to match.

Source: TechCrunch AI

3. Jev Becomes Vercel's Fastest-Adopted AI Model Ever

Vercel reports that Jev is its fastest-adopted AI model to date, underscoring how quickly developer tooling is integrating AI-native capabilities. The milestone reflects broader enterprise demand for AI that plugs directly into deployment workflows rather than sitting in a separate chat interface. It also signals that developer platforms are becoming key distribution channels for AI models.

Source: TechCrunch AI

4. Replit CEO: The Future of Work Is the "Self-Driving Company"

Replit's CEO argues that AI agents will soon run core business operations, creating what he calls the "self-driving company." In this vision, humans set direction while AI handles execution across coding, operations, and decision support. The claim reflects a growing belief that agentic AI will reshape org structures, not just individual workflows.

Source: TechCrunch AI

5. Elorian's First AI Model Reasons With Images, Not Just About Them

Elorian launched its debut AI model with a focus on reasoning with images rather than merely describing them. This distinction matters for robotics, design, and scientific applications where spatial and visual logic drive outcomes. It positions Elorian in the emerging "visual reasoning" category that could power next-gen multimodal agents.

Source: TechCrunch AI

6. Nvidia Veteran Turned VC: Physical AI Is More Than Robots

A former Nvidia insider now investing in physical AI argues the category extends well beyond humanoid robots. He points to sensors, simulation, industrial systems, and embodied software as equally important frontiers. His view suggests investors may be over-indexing on robotics while missing broader physical-AI opportunities.

Source: TechCrunch AI

7. Why Robotics Hasn't Hit Its ChatGPT Moment Yet

In a companion video, the same Nvidia veteran-turned-VC explains why robotics still lacks a breakthrough moment comparable to ChatGPT. He cites data scarcity, hardware costs, and the difficulty of generalizing across physical environments. The takeaway: physical AI will likely arrive gradually, not in a single viral leap.

Source: TechCrunch AI

8. Amazon Devices Chief: AI Devices Will Replace the Need for Apps

Panos Panay, Amazon's devices chief, predicts a future where AI devices eliminate the need to open apps entirely. Instead, users will express intent and let ambient AI handle tasks across services. If accurate, this shift would upend app-store economics and force developers to rethink distribution.

Source: TechCrunch AI

9. AI Agents Are Coming to Rivian's Cars

Rivian is bringing AI agents into its vehicles, joining a wave of automakers embedding conversational and agentic AI into the driving experience. The move points to cars becoming AI platforms, not just transportation hardware. It also raises questions about safety, latency, and how much control drivers will delegate.

Source: TechCrunch AI

10. Bluesky Expands Attie App to Let Anyone Build Websites and Games

Bluesky is expanding its AI-powered Attie app, enabling users to build websites, games, and more through natural language. The move pushes Bluesky beyond social networking into creation tools, directly competing with no-code and AI app builders. It's a notable pivot that could redefine the platform's identity.

Source: TechCrunch AI

Editor's Note

Today's news reveals a maturing AI industry pulling in two directions at once: deeper into the physical world through robotics, vehicles, and implants, and deeper into the software stack through open-weight models, enterprise agents, and developer tooling. The most consequential stories are those that lower barriers—Meta's on-device model, Anthropic's transparency move, and Vercel's rapid adoption curve—because they shape who gets to build with AI and under what conditions. The "self-driving company" and app-less device visions remain speculative, but the infrastructure to support them is being laid today.
