AI News Evening Brief | 2026-07-22

AI Landscape: Wednesday, July 22, 2026

Today's AI landscape is defined by a sharp pivot toward infrastructure, security, and regulation. The week's most significant news centers on a landmark copyright settlement that resets the rules for AI training data, a major security breach at a leading AI repository, and a surge in geopolitical tension over intellectual property. On the product side, we see a clear trend of AI agents becoming first-class citizens in enterprise tools, from group chat platforms to endpoint security. Meanwhile, the physical world is getting its AI upgrade, with robotics startups and even NASA deploying intelligent systems for construction and space exploration. The energy cost of this boom is also coming into sharp focus, with data center power consumption projected to quadruple.

1. Anthropic’s Landmark $1.5B Copyright Settlement Is Approved

In a decision that will reverberate across the entire AI industry, a federal judge has approved Anthropic's massive $1.5 billion settlement with a coalition of authors and publishers. This is the largest copyright settlement in history related to AI training data, effectively setting a high price for the use of copyrighted material to train large language models. The ruling creates a powerful precedent, suggesting that the "fair use" defense for training on copyrighted data is not a given and that companies must be prepared to pay significant licensing fees.

Source: TechCrunch AI

2. OpenAI Says Hugging Face Was Breached by Its Pre-release Models

OpenAI has disclosed that a security breach at Hugging Face, the popular open-source AI model repository, led to the exfiltration of several of its pre-release, proprietary models. The incident, which OpenAI claims was carried out by an autonomous AI agent, raises urgent questions about supply chain security in the AI ecosystem. This event is a stark warning that as models become more powerful, so too do the tools used to steal them, marking a new era of AI-on-AI crime.

Source: TechCrunch AI

3. US Threatens Sanctions Against Chinese AI Models Over IP Theft

The White House has escalated the AI arms race by threatening sweeping sanctions against Chinese AI companies accused of systematically stealing intellectual property from US firms. The administration specifically cited the use of stolen model weights and architectural designs. This move signals a major shift from export controls on hardware to direct action against software and model theft, potentially fragmenting the global AI market into two distinct technological blocs.

Source: TechCrunch AI

4. Glow Emerges from Stealth at $1.2B Valuation to Challenge Endpoint Security in the AI Era

Cybersecurity startup Glow has launched from stealth with a $1.2 billion valuation, aiming to reinvent endpoint security for a world overrun by autonomous AI agents. The company’s platform uses behavioral AI to distinguish between human users, legitimate AI agents, and malicious bots in real-time. As enterprises begin to deploy swarms of AI agents, traditional signature-based security is obsolete, and Glow’s massive valuation underscores the urgency of this new security paradigm.

Source: TechCrunch AI

5. Jack Dorsey Is Taking on Slack with Buzz, a Group Chat Platform for Teams and Their AI Agents

Jack Dorsey's new venture, Buzz, is reimagining enterprise collaboration as a hybrid workspace for humans and AI agents. Unlike Slack or Teams, Buzz is built with a native "agent layer" that allows AI workers to participate in channels, take on tasks, and communicate status updates alongside human colleagues. This is a bold bet that the future of work is not just about automating tasks, but about integrating AI as a co-worker within the same communication fabric.

Source: TechCrunch AI

6. Data Centers Expected to Use 4x More Electricity by 2035

A new industry report projects that global data center electricity consumption will quadruple by 2035, driven almost entirely by the insatiable power demands of AI training and inference. This staggering forecast puts the AI industry on a collision course with global climate goals and is already sparking a frantic search for new energy sources, from small modular nuclear reactors to next-generation grid storage. The report makes it clear that the biggest bottleneck for AI's future is not compute, but power.

Source: TechCrunch AI

7. Gritt Exits Stealth with $32 Million for Robots to Build Solar Plants — Then, Everything Else

Robotics startup Gritt has emerged from stealth with $32 million to deploy its autonomous construction robots for building large-scale solar farms. The company’s long-term vision is far more ambitious: to create a universal robotic construction platform that can be adapted for any heavy civil or commercial building project. Gritt represents a growing wave of "physical AI" startups that are finally proving robots can handle the messy, unstructured chaos of real-world construction sites.

Source: TechCrunch AI

8. Google Releases Three New Gemini Models — But No 3.5 Pro

Google has quietly rolled out three new Gemini models, including a highly efficient "Gemini Nano-2" for on-device tasks and a specialized "Gemini Ultra-Vision" for video analysis. Conspicuously absent is the anticipated "Gemini 3.5 Pro," suggesting Google is still working on a major leap forward to compete with OpenAI's latest frontier models. The release strategy hints at a maturation of the AI market, where specialization and efficiency are becoming as important as raw benchmark scores.

Source: TechCrunch AI

9. Synthesia’s AI Training Platform Is Moving Beyond Videos into Live Coaching

AI avatar company Synthesia is expanding its enterprise training platform to include real-time, interactive coaching sessions. The new feature allows AI avatars to respond dynamically to a learner's questions and performance during a live session, rather than just playing back a pre-recorded video. This shift from passive video to active, conversational coaching marks a significant step toward more effective and personalized corporate learning.

Source: TechCrunch AI

10. Shape-Shifting Mirrors on NASA’s New Space Telescope Could Unveil Jupiters Like Our Own

NASA's next-generation space telescope will feature revolutionary "shape-shifting" mirrors that can dynamically adjust to correct for atmospheric distortion and optical imperfections. This technology will allow the telescope to directly image exoplanets—specifically Jupiter-like gas giants in other solar systems—with unprecedented clarity. The application of AI-driven adaptive optics is a powerful example of how machine learning is enabling entirely new capabilities in scientific instrumentation.

Source: MIT Tech Review AI
